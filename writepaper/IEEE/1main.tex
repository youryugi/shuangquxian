We designed a visual annotation tool for hyperbolic ribbon-shaped targets, whose user interface is illustrated in the Fig.~\ref{fig:tool}.
First, the user selects an image folder, and the tool automatically loads all images within it.
The original image is displayed on the lower-left panel, while the annotated image is shown on the right.
A mouse click on the original image marks the vertex position of the hyperbola, and five adjustable parameters are provided on the right panel.

The five parameters are defined as follows: the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the hyperbola vertex, hyperbola width, height, and ribbon thickness.
By clicking the \emph{Add as New Annotation} button, the current parameter configuration is saved as a new annotation for the active image, and multiple annotations can be added to a single image.

All annotations are stored in JSON format with the following structure:
\begin{verbatim}

    {
      "label": "hyperbola",
      "x_vertex": 127.04,

      "y_vertex": 109.76,
      "width": 97.35,
      "height": 79.35,
      "thickness": 33.03
    }
  ]
}
\end{verbatim}

Here, \emph{024.jpg} denotes the image filename, and the remaining entries represent the parameters and their corresponding values.

\subsection{Overview}
Given a single-channel GPR B-scan $I \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times H\times W}$ (here $H=W=640$),
HyperbolaNet predicts a set of axis-aligned bounding boxes enclosing the hyperbolic
reflections. The network comprises three parts: a convolutional encoder backbone,
a \emph{supervised attention branch}, and a YOLO-style detection head.
The central contribution is the attention branch, whose attention map is explicitly
supervised by a rasterized hyperbola band mask \emph{inside the backbone}, and is then
injected into the bottleneck feature by channel concatenation. An overview is shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}.

\subsection{Encoder backbone}
The encoder consists of three down-sampling stages followed by a bottleneck. Each stage is
a convolutional block---two $3 \times 3$ convolutions, each followed by batch normalization (BN)
and ReLU---followed by $2 \times 2$ max pooling, so that the channel depth doubles and the
spatial resolution halves at each stage. The resulting feature dimensions
($\text{channels} \times H \times W$) are
$1 \times 640 \times 640$, $32 \times 320 \times 320$, $64 \times 160 \times 160$,
$128 \times 160 \times 160$, and $256 \times 80 \times 80$ at the bottleneck.
We denote by $f_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{C_3 \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}}$ the output of
the third stage \emph{before} its pooling ($C_3 = 128$, stride $4$), and by
$F_b \in \mathbb{R}^{C_b \times \frac{H}{8} \times \frac{W}{8}}$ the bottleneck feature
($C_b = 256$, stride $8$). The attention branch taps $f_3$ rather than $F_b$ because it
retains a higher spatial resolution.

\subsection{Supervised attention branch}

\paragraph{Attention map.}
A lightweight head $g_a(\cdot)$---a $3 \times 3$ convolution with BN and ReLU followed by a
$1 \times 1$ convolution---maps $f_3$ to a single-channel logit $z = g_a(f_3)$, and a sigmoid
$\sigma(\cdot)$ yields the attention map
\begin{equation}
A = \sigma\big(g_a(f_3)\big) \in (0,1)^{\,1 \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}} .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Band-mask construction.}
For each annotated hyperbola, parameterized by its vertex $(x_v,y_v)$, width $w$, height $h$,
and thickness $t$, we rasterize a ground-truth band mask. The central curve is the parabola
\begin{equation}
y_c(x) = y_v + h \left(\frac{x-x_v}{w/2}\right)^{2},
\qquad x \in \left[\, x_v-\tfrac{w}{2},\; x_v+\tfrac{w}{2} \,\right],
\end{equation}
and the band is the set of pixels within a vertical distance $t/2$ of this curve,
\begin{equation}
B(x,y) = \ind\left[\, \big| y - y_c(x) \big| \le \tfrac{t}{2} \,\right] .
\end{equation}
The per-image mask is the union over all hyperbolae in the image, down-sampled to the
resolution of $A$ and binarized.

\paragraph{Attention loss.}
The attention map is supervised to match $B$ pixel-wise using a combination of binary
cross-entropy and a Dice term. With $A_i, B_i \in [0,1]$ the values at pixel $i$, $N$ the
number of pixels, and a small constant $\epsilon$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bce}}
  &= -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\Big[\, B_i \log A_i + (1-B_i)\log(1-A_i) \,\Big], \\[2pt]
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dice}}
  &= 1 - \frac{2\sum_i A_i B_i}{\sum_i A_i + \sum_i B_i + \epsilon}, \\[2pt]
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{att}}
  &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bce}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dice}} .
\end{align}
Crucially, this supervision is applied to a feature map \emph{within the backbone}, not to
the detection head, so it steers the encoder to concentrate its response on the hyperbola
band region.

\subsection{Attention-guided fusion}
To inject the attention prior into the detection pathway, $A$ is average-pooled by a factor
of two to align with the bottleneck resolution,
$\tilde{A} = \mathrm{AvgPool}_{2}(A) \in (0,1)^{\,1 \times \frac{H}{8} \times \frac{W}{8}}$,
concatenated with $F_b$ along the channel axis, and reduced back to $C_b$ channels by a
$1 \times 1$ convolution $\phi(\cdot)$ (with BN and ReLU):
\begin{equation}
\hat{F} = \phi\big( [\, F_b \,;\, \tilde{A} \,] \big)
        \in \mathbb{R}^{\,C_b \times \frac{H}{8} \times \frac{W}{8}} ,
\end{equation}
where $[\, \cdot \,;\, \cdot \,]$ denotes channel concatenation (yielding $C_b + 1$ channels).

\subsection{Detection head and decoding}
Three parallel heads operate on $\hat{F}$ at stride $s = 8$ over an
$\frac{H}{s} \times \frac{W}{s}$ grid: an objectness head producing logits $O$, a size head
producing normalized width/height $\hat{S} \in (0,1)^{2}$ (sigmoid-activated), and an offset
head producing sub-cell center offsets $\hat{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$. For each grid cell
$(i,j)$ whose objectness $\sigma(O_{ij})$ exceeds a threshold $\tau$, the box center and size
in image coordinates are
\begin{align}
c_x &= \big(j + \mathrm{clip}(\hat{D}^{x}_{ij},-0.5,0.5)\big)\, s, &
c_y &= \big(i + \mathrm{clip}(\hat{D}^{y}_{ij},-0.5,0.5)\big)\, s, \\
b_w &= \hat{S}^{w}_{ij}\, W, & b_h &= \hat{S}^{h}_{ij}\, H,
\end{align}
giving the box $[\, c_x - \tfrac{b_w}{2},\; c_y - \tfrac{b_h}{2},\; c_x + \tfrac{b_w}{2},\;
c_y + \tfrac{b_h}{2} \,]$. Overlapping candidates are removed by box-level IoU
non-maximum suppression.

\subsection{Training objective}
Let $P \in \{0,1\}$ be the hard objectness target ($P_{ij} = 1$ at the cell containing an
object center, $0$ otherwise), $N_{+} = \max(\sum_{ij} P_{ij}, 1)$,
$N_{-} = \max(\sum_{ij}(1 - P_{ij}), 1)$, and
$\ell_{ij} = \mathrm{BCE}(O_{ij}, P_{ij})$ the per-cell objectness loss. The objectness
term balances positives and negatives separately,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obj}}
  = \frac{1}{N_{+}}\sum_{ij} P_{ij}\, \ell_{ij}
  \;+\; \lambda_{\mathrm{noobj}}\, \frac{1}{N_{-}}\sum_{ij}(1 - P_{ij})\, \ell_{ij},
\end{equation}
and the size and offset terms are masked $L_1$ losses evaluated only at positive cells,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{size}}
  = \frac{1}{N_{+}}\sum_{ij} P_{ij}\, \big\lVert \hat{S}_{ij} - S^{*}_{ij} \big\rVert_1,
\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}
  = \frac{1}{N_{+}}\sum_{ij} P_{ij}\, \big\lVert \hat{D}_{ij} - D^{*}_{ij} \big\rVert_1,
\end{equation}
where $S^{*}$ and $D^{*}$ are the ground-truth normalized size and sub-cell offset. The full
training objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obj}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{size}}
            + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{att}}\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{att}},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{noobj}} = 0.5$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{att}}$ a weight that balances the
attention supervision against the detection terms. Only the attention loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{att}}$ is added when the supervised branch is enabled; the baseline
without attention sets $\lambda_{\mathrm{att}} = 0$ and removes the branch.
