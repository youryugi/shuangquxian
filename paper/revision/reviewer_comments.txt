Reviewer(s) Comments:
Reviewer: 1

Comments to the Author
This paper addresses the problem of detecting target hyperbolas in ground-penetrating radar (GPR) B-scan images by proposing a method based on hyperbola-banded attention supervision. Using the HBAT annotation tool developed in this study, the method employs the geometric characteristics of four interpretable parameters—vertex, slope, span, and thickness—to parameterize the curves, generate strip-shaped masks to display supervisory signals as attention branches, and improve the detection performance of buried targets through end-to-end training with limited annotated data. The following suggestions are provided for the authors’ reference during revisions.
1. Since the hyperbolic strip region serves as an auxiliary supervision mechanism, conduct validation experiments based on core hypotheses to determine whether the ultimate performance improvement stems primarily from the hyperbolic geometric priors themselves or from the added pixel-level supervision task, thereby demonstrating the independent contribution of the geometric supervision mechanism.
2. The hyperbolic supervision regions are generated parametrically as strip-shaped regions of fixed width. Assess the adaptability of these parameters under varying conditions in real-world radar scenarios—such as differences in frequency, background media, and target media—and when reflection characteristics vary. Specifically, evaluate their applicability when reflection curves are incomplete, overlapping, distorted, or subject to noise interference due to factors like heterogeneous backgrounds, terrain undulations, or complex targets. Determine whether the geometric priors employed possess adaptation mechanisms suitable for such complex real-world scenarios.
3. The paper compares different attention modules; it is recommended to include a comparison with representative GPR target detection methods.
4. As HBAT is one of the contributions of this paper, it is recommended to strengthen the explanation and analysis regarding how consistency in annotated data is ensured under different annotation schemes, the sensitivity analysis of how changes in annotation parameters affect the final detection performance, and whether parameters can be reasonably calculated for complex hyperbolas.


Reviewer: 2

Comments to the Author
The manuscript presents a potentially useful annotation and supervision approach. However, its methodological significance and practical advantage under difficult GPR conditions are not yet convincingly established:
1. Section IV-A excludes samples without clearly visible wings on both sides, while Section IV-F selects intact pipeline reflections. This selection removes cases central to the stated motivation. Please evaluate a representative set of weak, incomplete, and distorted hyperbolas.
2. Many hyperbolas in Figs. 4–5 remain visually discernible despite surrounding interference. Please quantify performance across different visibility and clutter levels, and include failure cases rather than relying on manually selected successful examples.
3. The advance over existing geometry-aware methods remains insufficiently established. Please clarify the distinction from GPR-Former and geometric-attention approaches discussed in Section II, and compare against suitable complete detectors rather than only substituted attention modules.
4. HBAT requires manual adjustment of several parameters. Please report annotation time, inter-annotator agreement, and sensitivity to band thickness and span. Comparisons at equal annotation-time budgets would better support the claimed value under limited labeling resources.
5. Random image splitting may not ensure independent evaluation if images originate from related survey lines or overlapping crops. Please document image provenance and separate related observations by survey, site, or original radargram where applicable.
6. The training-ratio experiments also change the test-set size and composition. Please use a fixed independent test set and progressively increase the training subset to assess data efficiency more clearly.
7. Table V already demonstrates poor cross-dataset transfer, with the proposed method providing no improvement over the baseline. Please distinguish benefits obtained after retraining from transferable performance, and moderate the claim that the gains are independent of dataset characteristics.
8. Reporting ten random seeds is valuable, but paired performance differences and confidence intervals would better establish the reliability of the gains. Please also report uncertainty for Table IV and qualify stability claims where Table II shows little improvement or increased variability.
9. Section IV-E compares positive-λ medians with the baseline value of 0.68, which Table II reports as a mean. Please reconcile these summaries and use consistent statistics when interpreting Fig. 6.
10. The selection of clearly identifiable hyperbolas weakens the evidence supporting detection of weak or incomplete reflections.
